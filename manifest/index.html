The cofounder manifest

Built for builders.

The inspiration is yours.

There’s something you want to exist. You can picture it. You care enough to make it happen. No agent can take that away from you.

We’re building cofounder to help you turn that inspiration into working software, with an AI team you can use from your browser.

Setup is our job.

You shouldn’t have to spend your weekend configuring agents, connecting tools, and assembling a harness. We take responsibility for making the whole thing work, from the first plan to the finished change.

Good software has opinions.

We choose the tools and how they work together. Every setting is another decision we’re asking you to make. It had better be worth it. When a better tool comes along, we’ll change our minds.

“Done” needs proof.

Agree on what should happen. Write tests that catch mistakes. Have another agent challenge the work. Show the result and what was checked. If something failed, say so. Remember the lesson next time.

Make it a pleasure.

Readable text. Room to breathe. A clear next step. Ask for your attention when a decision needs your judgment. Take care of the routine work. Powerful software should feel good to use.

You stay in charge.

You decide what matters and when to change course. The code and the project’s history should remain yours to inspect, change, and take with you. Good defaults are a starting point. You get the final say.

Our compass: the harness DHH would build. Our interpretation of his product philosophy and Omarchy: choose carefully, make the parts work together, and care about every detail. We judge every decision by that standard.

Ideas we build on

Studied methodologies, adapted through production experience:

  • DHH’s Rails Doctrine and Omarchy: thoughtful defaults, integrated tools, craft, and ownership.
  • spec-kit: constitution, clarify before planning, separate WHAT from HOW.
  • superpowers: two-stage review, evidence before claims, TDD iron law.
  • gstack: adapted quote-the-evidence verification and file-anchored learnings.
  • OpenSpec: numbered WHEN/THEN scenarios as the TDD coverage contract.
  • Minto’s The Pyramid Principle: MECE proposals and scenarios, without overlaps or gaps.
  • Adversarial-critics TDD: refute coverage claims and check that tests catch deliberate mutations.
  • hyper-code: persistent agent workspaces, tools, and artifacts together.

We’re in early development. This is the standard we’re building toward.
