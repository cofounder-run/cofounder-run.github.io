The cofounder manifest

Built for builders.

The harness DHH would build.

That’s our guiding question for every decision: what would a powerful, opinionated AI harness look like if it were designed as one complete product for builders?

Our interpretation, inspired by DHH’s product philosophy and Omarchy. Your inspiration is yours. No agent can take that from you. Spend your energy on what you want to build. We take care of the agents and the harness, ready to use in your browser.

Choose well. Make it work together.

We choose the tools, connect the workflow, and build in the discipline. Planning, design, code, review, and release should feel like one product. Good defaults should get you straight to building.

Make room for the builder.

Show what matters: the goal, the progress, the result, and the decisions that need you. Take care of routine work. Give every screen breathing room. Make the whole experience coherent, beautiful, and a pleasure to use.

Earn the word “done.”

Clarify the goal. Define success through concrete scenarios. Test the behavior, challenge the implementation, and show the evidence. Keep failures visible and carry lessons into the next task.

Strong defaults. Real ownership.

Omarchy’s omakase approach is our inspiration: a carefully assembled starting point that remains yours to change. Keep the builder in control of the direction, the code, and the decision to leave.

Keep the judgment. Evolve the tools.

Choose languages, models, and architecture for the job. Revisit them when evidence changes. Put agents to work throughout the process. Every feature must earn its complexity through a better result for the builder.

Ideas we build on

Studied methodologies, adapted through production experience:

  • DHH’s Rails Doctrine and Omarchy: thoughtful defaults, integrated tools, craft, and ownership.
  • spec-kit: constitution, clarify before planning, separate WHAT from HOW.
  • superpowers: two-stage review, evidence before claims, TDD iron law.
  • gstack: adapted quote-the-evidence verification and file-anchored learnings.
  • OpenSpec: numbered WHEN/THEN scenarios as the TDD coverage contract.
  • Minto’s The Pyramid Principle: MECE proposals and scenarios, without overlaps or gaps.
  • Adversarial-critics TDD: refute coverage claims and check that tests catch deliberate mutations.
  • hyper-code: persistent agent workspaces, tools, and artifacts together.

We’re in early development. This is the standard we’re building toward.
